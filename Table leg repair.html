A sofa table from my mother-in-law's estate was requested by my son and daughter-in-law living a few hours away. The table was brought home, discovering a leg had been chewed on, presumably by a dog. It is an old table from an antique dealer. I will attempt a repair of the dog gnawed table leg.

The first approach is by building up the missing bits with wood putty. A new tube of wood putty was purchased, Minwax Stainable Wood Filler, and the first layer applied. After letting this thick layer dry overnight a second layer was added. The first photo below shows the first layer. The second shows the still wet second layer.

A profile gauge was used to capture the profile of the leg. Screws were loosened and while the gauge was held against the leg the wires were pressed into position. After tightening the screws the profile was transfered to a scrap of 1/4" poplar. The profile was cut on a jig saw and then refined with multiple applications of needle files as it was held against the leg. The custom sanding block is a close match.

Double sided tape was used to apply 120 grit sandpaper to the sanding block. The hardened wood putty was sanded with files, flat and round as well as the sanding block as the filing got closer to the desired profile. A few divots appeared and an a bit more wood putty was applied. This was then sanded to the desired profile with the sanding block and lightly sanded with 220 grit sandpaper.

Stain was applied, both a lighter redder stain for the bulging area and a darker walnut stain for the rest. There was much less contrast than desired. Luckily this is a back leg. The photo below shows the stained leg.

Wipe-on polyurethane was chosen for the final coat, mostly because it is not very glossy. 2-3 hours is required between coats. Three coats were applied and the table was delivered.
