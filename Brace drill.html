A quick project is in order this morning. It is not clear how much I can get done as there are workmen here in the house. The short version of a long story follows. We decided to put hardwood in four rooms on the first floor, the family room and den on one side of the kitchen and entryway and the living room and dining room on the other. Back in September carpet was removed. Two issues were revealed. The subfloor and a joist below the living room window were rotting! The second issue discovered is that the floors are "hilly", the floor joists were not installed level!

Getting the two problems repaired has been challenging. A carpenter was out last week and fixed the rotten joist. The company laying the hardwood is out today with a crew that has experience repairing the hilly floors. This morning was ripping up the old subfloor. Then comes leveling the joists, lower some and shim others. The noise is intense. The house, already a mess from things removed from the four rooms and scattered here and there, now has even more removed and plastic covers everything on the first floor. Had to order out for lunch and eat it in the basement. A photo of the part of the living room as seen from the stairs is shown below.

The quick project selected was reconditioning a ratcheting brace drill, shown below. The ear plugs are back in as I write up this project. Three other ancient rusty tools also awaiting restoration are seen in the second photo below.

The round handle was removed first. Three screws held it to the metal flange. The flange was held on by a clip on the end of the rod. The clip was slid out and the flange came off. Behind this flange was a race of ball bearings. Only one was lost when the flange came off!

Unscrewing the sleeve that constrains the drill's jaws revealed the jaw setup, both jaws held by spring steel wire. A pin holds this wire in place and was knocked out with a pin punch. The jaws were wiggled off of the steel wire. Knocking out the pin allowed the wire spring to be removed.

Two pins hold the ratchet pawls in place. These pins were knocked out with a pin punch, both slightly longer than the first pin removed. When the pawls came out a compression spring bounced free, luckily it was not lost.

Three things were cleaned up first using a wire wheel in the drill press, the C-shaped handle, the jaw sleeve, and the flange. All are seen below after cleaning.

The two plastic handles were sanded. The cylindrical handle was sanded first with 80 grit sandpaper as it was quite pockmarked. It was sanded to 400 grit. The round handle was sanded first with 220 grit and then 320 and 400 grit. The smaller items were cleaned with the wire wheel. All cleaned parts are shown in the third photo below. A few of the steel parts were further buffed with a 3M sanding disk before the photo was taken.

Reassembly began with the bearings. A bit of black lithium grease was placed in the bearing race. The bearings were dropped in. The flange was put in place as was the clip. The plastic handle was screwed into place after buffing the screw heads with the 3M sanding disk. At this point the round handle and the cylindrical handle were wiped down using a rag wet with acetone, C3H6O (the chemist in me couldn't resist). This greatly improved their looks.

Too much time was spent trying to get the two pawls and their spring back in place. Eventually, the obvious thing was tried. The sleeve that serves to switch the ratchet direction is stopped at two positions 180° apart by a very short pin. This pin was pulled out with a pair of pliers, allowing the sleeve to slide down and uncovering the slot for the pawls. The pawls with spring were put in place with one pin and the sleeve slid into place. The second pin was installed. The small pin serving as sleeve retainer and stop was tapped back into place. A second pin holding the head to the handle could not be tapped out, though at this point there was no need to remove the head of the brace.

The jaw sleeve was lubricated with 3-in-1 oil and installed after putting the jaws, spring, and pin in position. Two photos of the restored brace drill are included below. The brace just needs a bit and a project requiring holes.
